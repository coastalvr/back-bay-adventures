Capt. Robert L. BrodieProfessional hunting & fishing guide, award-winning outdoor writer and photographer · Biloxi, Mississippi

So what does a guy from Kentucky, with a penchant for big game, do on the Florida Panhandle? After years of offshore fishing I began the quest of tarpon fishing. Having heard for years that big tarpon cruise the sugar sand beaches out of casting distance from the beach and too far in to be of concern to the offshore fleet, the Silver Kings slip by almost unnoticed.

Three years ago I attempted a tarpon trip only to be beaten by the weather and unable to make it out of the harbor in the typically small flats boats. This year was different. After completing a successful offshore trip including a boat limit of red snapper and king mackerel the day before, I met my guide, Captain Todd Jones of Back Bay Adventures, at what he described as "brutally early": 4:30 in the morning.

In the preliminary phone conversations Todd shared the lay of the land: don't get your hopes up, tarpon can be tough, don't set the hook too quick, and only 1 out of 20 hookups makes it to the boat. Capt. Todd is honest at his profession just like all good guides. Honest, hard working for his clients, and gets as much if not more enjoyment than they do at the hook set.

The morning started off with catching live bait, then a short run to the tarpon grounds. The first tarpon was spotted just seconds after our 6:00 am arrival, followed by a steady flow of fish until almost the noon hour. I can only compare tarpon fishing to elk hunting: spot and stalk big game. Spot the tarpon gulping air or rolling at the surface, determine a line of interception, present your bait and hope for the best. When the best happens, hang on for the ride of your life!

Fishing is fishing, sometimes the bite is on, sometimes it's not. This day happened to be one of the days it was light, resulting in only a single hookup. No reflection on the guide or location; there were plenty of fish and presentations, just not a lot of takers that day. Proudly I can say that Captain Todd and I were the only boat with a hookup and jump in the area that day, and yes, there were other boats.

It may seem that I'm downplaying the single hookup. It was phenomenal! A light touch as the bait was taken and allowed to free spool, a moment of steadiness as the hook grabbed the fleshy corner of the fish's mouth, followed by an explosion of energy only rivaled by a big sail or marlin: the classic, stuff-dreams-are-made-of leap and cartwheels from 150 lbs of pure, raw, unbridled strength. With the fish's tail two feet above the surface, a cartwheel and a spray of water, the bridle slipped from its mouth. I'm sure that fish felt the same as I did: excited, flushed and looking for another fight.

Back Bay Adventures knows the area and where the fish are. They are professional and enjoy the sport of sight fishing big fish. You can't ask for anything more; I know I can't. I look forward to the hunt again with Capt. Todd.

Mark NetheryBluegrass Game Calls · bluegrassgamecalls.com
